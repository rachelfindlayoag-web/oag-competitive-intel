Acknowledge our gaps honestly

Gate coverage (56% vs Cirium 69%), emissions certification, ancillary data, and Analyser UI maturity are real. Own them — it builds trust. Then pivot to where OAG genuinely wins.

The question that wins most deals

"What decisions are you trying to make with this data?" Positional tracking is a different job to commercial intelligence. Most competitors cover one layer. OAG covers the full stack.

The one thing no competitor can say

OAG is the only provider with schedules + demand + pricing + status in one platform. Cirium has no pricing. FlightAware and FR24 have no commercial layer. IATA SDEP has no analytics.

What IATA SDEP actually is
Program overview
Launched August 2025. Industry-owned, airline-governed schedule and MCT database. Free give-to-get model — airlines share their schedules, receive the combined database back. Delivers global SSIM files, flat files, or cloud-native tables via SFTP. Positioned explicitly as "an industry initiative, not a commercial solution." No analytics, no real-time status, no pricing, no demand data, no visualisation tools.
The real threat — supply, not demand
Where the risk sits
The threat is not that airlines will buy SDEP instead of OAG. They cannot — SDEP does not replace OAG's products. The risk is that SDEP reduces airline dependence on commercial aggregators for raw schedule data over time, potentially weakening OAG's schedule supply relationships. Airlines may also use SDEP language to negotiate harder on schedule data pricing.
Where OAG wins vs IATA SDEP
  • Full commercial analytics platform — SDEP has none
  • Real-time status tracking — SDEP has none
  • Pricing and demand data — SDEP has none
  • Cloud delivery with analytics tools (Analyser, APIs) — SDEP only delivers raw files
  • 90+ years of quality assurance and processing depth
  • 24-month forward schedules vs basic SSIM output from SDEP
  • 15-minute refresh cycle vs SDEP's static file distribution
Where IATA SDEP has leverage
  • Free to participating airlines — creates a price pressure argument
  • Industry governance — hard to argue against "airline-owned" positioning
  • 150+ airlines already signed — broad and growing participation
  • IATA brand authority — trusted by default in airline procurement
Handle the objections
"IATA is giving this away for free"Clarify what free actually means. "IATA's program gives you raw schedule files. That is a backup, not a product. It has no analytics, no status tracking, no pricing data, no query tools. OAG takes that raw material and turns it into something you can actually make commercial decisions with."
"We want to reduce dependence on data aggregators""That is understandable. But aggregator dependence and commercial intelligence are different things. OAG is not just an aggregator — we enrich, validate, and deliver actionable intelligence at a depth and speed that SDEP simply does not match."
"IATA has airline governance — that matters to us""OAG has a formal IATA partnership and processes data to IATA's own standards. We are also the organisation that defined the SSIM format that SDEP is built on. Our relationship with IATA is collaborative, not competitive."
"We are already signed up to SDEP"This is fine — they are not mutually exclusive. "SDEP and OAG solve different problems. SDEP gives you a raw schedule backup. OAG gives you the intelligence layer on top — status, demand, pricing, and analytics. Most airlines will use both."
